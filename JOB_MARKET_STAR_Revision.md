# Job Market Trend Analysis — STAR Revision Table (CRISP-ML(Q) sequence)

**Project one-liner:** Self-initiated market-intelligence analysis of 607 freelance data-job
postings (2020–2022) to help freelancers benchmark salaries, spot in-demand roles, and
quantify the remote-work premium — cleaned & analyzed in Python, delivered as Power BI
dashboards (Freelance Analysis.pbix + Job_Market.pbix).

**Dataset card:** 607 rows | 11 cols | 0 nulls | 42 duplicates found | years 2020–2022 |
salary_in_usd mean $112,298 / median $101,570 / std $70,957 / range $597K |
remote_ratio mean 70.9 (median 100 = fully remote).

## STAR TABLE — Situation | Action | Impact | Learning

| # | Step (CRISP phase) | SITUATION (why) | ACTION (what exactly we did) | IMPACT (result/number) | LEARNING |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Business Understanding** — problem framing | Freelancers lack transparency on pay, can't position skills; need data answers to "which roles pay, and does remote work pay more?" | Wrote formal Business Problem doc (`Freelance Business Problem.txt`): objectives (trend identification, skill-gap analysis, client-preference mapping), constraints (no real-time multi-platform API access, inconsistent formats, changing platform policies), success criteria (80% geo coverage) | Project scope locked to 3 objectives + measurable success criteria; every analysis block mapped to an objective | Writing objectives/constraints/success criteria FIRST prevents scope creep on open-ended "explore the market" projects |
| 2 | **Data Understanding** — acquisition | Live data only available behind platforms' anti-bot walls | Attempted scraping: Freelancer.com via **BeautifulSoup** (worked — static HTML); Upwork failed (client-side React rendering + CAPTCHA + anti-bot → account risk). **Pivoted to public datasets** (Kaggle/HuggingFace): loaded `Freelance_data.xlsx` | Clean, legal, reproducible 607-row dataset acquired without ToS violations — a complete "story" of Attempt → Challenge → Responsible Pivot | Ethics & reproducibility matter: when live scraping risks platform violations, public datasets are the professional choice — a great interview talking point |
| 3 | **Data Understanding** — profiling & 4-moment business decisions | Must understand the shape/distribution of salaries before recommending anything | `shape`, `info()`, `describe()`; then **Four-Moment Business Decisions** per numeric col: 1st moment mean/median/mode; 2nd std/variance/range; 3rd skewness; 4th kurtosis (`Freelance_Analysis.ipynb`) | salary_in_usd: mean $112K vs median $102K (right-tailed); skew +1.67, kurtosis +6.35; raw salary skew 14.05 / kurtosis 247 (extreme currency mixing); remote_ratio skew −0.90 | The 4-moment method is a systematic "first read" of any numeric data; mean vs median gap immediately flags skewed compensation data |
| 4 | **Data Preparation** — quality audit | Job boards notoriously double-list the same role with different formatting — averages get skewed | `isnull().sum()` → 0 nulls verified per column; `duplicated().sum()` → **42 duplicates found**; categorical uniqueness scan (`unique()` per object col); IQR outlier scan per numeric col | Established clean baseline: 0 missing but 7% of rows (42/607) redundant; outliers: salary 44, salary_in_usd 10 (kept & examined, not deleted) | Duplicate listings silently inflate demand counts — dedup is non-negotiable before any market-share claim |
| 5 | **Data Preparation** — cleaning & preprocessing | Raw postings not model/dashboard-ready | `df.drop_duplicates()`; LabelEncoder for categorical cols; StandardScaler on numerics; engineered feature `title_word_count` (word count of job_title) | Modeling-ready dataset; new derived feature enables title-length analysis | Preprocessing = drop dups → encode → scale → feature-engineer; a standard repeatable sequence |
| 6 | **Data Analysis** — EDA structure scan | Which dimensions deserve segmentation? | `value_counts()` on experience_level, employment_type, company_size; unique-value inventory of all object columns; numeric correlation matrix + heatmap | Segmentation lineup decided: EN 88 / MI 213 / SE 280 / EX 26; FT 588 / PT 10 / CT 5 / FL 4; company M 326 / L 198 / S 83; correlations all weak (max salary_in_usd ↔ remote_ratio = 0.13) | Correlation scan told us single variables won't "explain" salary — segmentation & grouping would |
| 7 | **Data Analysis** — trends & YoY | Is the market growing? Which roles are rising vs fading? | `groupby(work_year, job_title)` top jobs per year; unstacked year×role matrix; `pct_change()` **year-over-year growth**; time-series trend plot | Role demand rankings by year; growth momentum per role quantified | YoY via groupby+unstack+pct_change — a compact pandas pattern to memorize |
| 8 | **Data Analysis** — salary segmentation (the core payoff) | Freelancers' #1 question: where does money concentrate? | `groupby(experience_level)['salary_in_usd'].describe()`; groupby remote_ratio describe; groupby country median salary (nlargest 10); bar/box/count/pie plots (`Masviualization` cells) | **Salary by level: EX $199K, SE $138.6K, MI $88K, EN $61.6K** (3.3× gap entry→executive); by work model **Remote $122,458 / On-site $106,355 / Hybrid $80,823**; top countries led by US | Median + describe-per-group beats single averages; bar/box visuals make the compensation gradient obvious |
| 9 | **Data Analysis** — remote-work analysis | Remote was the market's headline shift post-2020 — does it hold in freelance data? | Grouped remote_ratio (0/50/100 = on-site/hybrid/remote); `value_counts(normalize=True)` per year → **remote trend over years**; stacked time-series over 2020–2022 | **Remote = 62.8%** (381 of 607 posts); **remote Premium = +15.1% vs on-site, +51.5% vs hybrid**; remote share at SE level 71.1% vs MI 54% | normalized=True gives percentage splits instantly; the premium varies by seniority, so "remote pays more" statement needs a level caveat |
| 10 | **Data Analysis** — role & spend concentration | Which careers deserve the skilling investment? | Role rankings by count & by total salary spend; top-countries salary median ranking | **Data Scientist 143 posts & Data Engineer 132 posts lead demand**; their salary spend $15.5M + $14.9M ≈ **44.5% of the total $68.2M** pool — strong concentration signal | Combining demand (count) and spend (salary×count) shows "where the market's money flows," stronger than demand alone |
| 11 | **Evaluation / sanity checks** | Are findings distortion-free (dups removed, currencies normalized)? | Extracted metadata (extract_metadata.py in project folder family), cross-checked currency-normalized USD column vs raw salary (why salary_in_usd skew 1.67 vs raw salary skew 14/247 — mixing currencies); audited duplicate removal impact | Confirmed analysis on 565 unique postings after dedup; USD-normalization is what makes cross-country salary comparison fair | Always normalize currencies before cross-country comparisons — raw `salary` col was unusable, `salary_in_usd` is the analysis column |
| 12 | **Deployment** — Power BI dashboards | Insights must be explorable by a non-technical freelancer/client | Built **`Freelance Analysis.pbix`** and **`Job_Market.pbix`**: salary-by-experience columns, role demand bars, remote-ratio distribution donut/pie, country salary ranking, YoY trend lines, KPI cards (avg salary, postings count, remote share) | Portfolio-facing interactive delivery of salary benchmarks and remote-premium findings | Dashboards translate statistical findings into one-glance answers; match chart type to question (bar=rank, line=trend, pie=share) |
| 13 | **Monitoring / scalability** (concept) | Job markets move fast — a static snapshot ages quickly | Dataset constraint documented; refresh path = re-ingest updated public dataset re-running the same notebook + .pbix refresh | Project designed as repeatable: same notebook re-run produces updated dashboards | Analyst deliverables age; build a refresh path and state dataset limits honestly in interviews |

After my Upwork scraping ran into anti-bot walls, I pivoted to public datasets for an ethical, reproducible pipeline. I shipped the findings as two Power BI dashboards so anyone can explore pay by role, level, country, and work model."
